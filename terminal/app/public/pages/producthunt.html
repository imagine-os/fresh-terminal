Product Hunt strategy

How Fresh Terminal launches on Product Hunt. Launch the product people can use today, tell the FreshStack story inside it, and go only when the blockers below are green. Nothing here is scheduled; the order is set by dependencies, not dates.

The call

Launch Fresh Terminal first, not FreshStack. Product Hunt's featuring guidelines (2026-03-10) list templates and boilerplates among things it does not feature, and products on the same root domain share one six-month gap between launches. A starter kit posted on its own would probably land in the unfeatured "All" feed and use up the domain's slot.

  1. Lead with what nothing else does: you type, the terminal changes itself, and every change can be undone or replayed. That is live today and shows in a three-second clip.
  2. FreshStack is the story in the maker's comment and the GitHub link. It grows on GitHub and developer forums, not on the leaderboard. It gets its own launch later only if it becomes a working product (for example, one command that deploys a live multiplayer game) on its own domain.
  3. Five blockers before scheduling: Clerk production sign-in (development is capped at 100 users), free credits with a per-browser cap, a launch-day spend limit on our key, a first-run screen that says where your work is saved (shipped 03:25 UTC, C-072), and a licence on the repo (it has none, so "open" can't be claimed yet). Four are still open.
  4. Don't claim realtime multiplayer on launch day. SpacetimeDB isn't published yet. Save "game-first realtime" for the second launch, where it counts as the significant update Product Hunt requires.

Pitch and tagline

Product Hunt's tagline can be 60 characters at most, and the description 260. Their guide asks for plain and descriptive over clever. "Evolve as we grow." stays our brand line on the site and at the end of the maker's comment; it doesn't say what the product does, so it isn't the Product Hunt tagline.

      Timeline by dependencies

      No dates. Each task starts when the tasks it names are done. The model follows the house rule: Fable for judgment and shared code, Opus 5 for building, Sonnet 5 for mechanical passes. Launch day starts at 12:01 AM Pacific, when the Product Hunt day begins. Tuesday to Thursday is folklore; Product Hunt says the best day is the one you're ready for.

      Community plan

      The rule: we never ask for upvotes. Product Hunt says asking can push a product down the ranking or take it off the homepage. We ask people to try it and tell us what breaks. Points count upvotes and meaningful engagement, like comments, so honest comments help and a vote ring hurts.

      Before

      Justin's personal Product Hunt account (company accounts aren't allowed), with a complete maker profile, commenting on other launches for a few weeks. We add a "tell me on launch day" email field to our own site, because Product Hunt ended its Coming Soon pages around August 2025. Post in Product Hunt's forums (/p/) about the build, not the launch. Share build clips on X and LinkedIn that link to the site, not to Product Hunt.

      Launch day

      At 12:01 AM Pacific: the maker's comment goes up, the email goes out, and a banner appears on the site. One personal note per person on our list, saying "we're live, try it, tell us what breaks," and never "please upvote." Two people answer comments in shifts all day, aiming for under 15 minutes. Bugs reported in comments get fixed the same day, and we reply with the changelog link.

      Where developers already are

      The FreshStack story goes to GitHub (README, topics, a pinned "start here" discussion), a Show HN on a different day, and one or two forums where the stack's own communities gather (SpacetimeDB, LiveKit, Cloudflare). We post only when we have something to show, not to promote the launch.

      What we don't do

      No paid hunters (Product Hunt says there's no advantage), no upvote exchanges, no bought votes, no mass DMs, no incentives for votes, no posting from new or company accounts. Friends who have never used Product Hunt should comment with something real, or not at all.

      Hi Product Hunt, I'm Justin, and I make Fresh Terminal.

      I kept rebuilding the same dashboard for every business I run, and every rebuild was a week of waiting on someone else's tools. So we built a terminal that rebuilds itself instead.

      You type what you want, like "make the sidebar a rail", "switch to Glass Window" or "add a Projects menu", and the interface changes in front of you. Every change is a step you can undo, or scrub back through in Replay.

      What you can try right now:

      • Open a skinned terminal from the Library. The koi pond is my favourite.
      • Click a word chip to tell it what you mean: "Hoy" the brand, or "hoy" the day.
      • Watch what every reply costs in the top bar. We pass the model cost straight through, or you can paste your own OpenRouter key, which never leaves your browser.
      • [Free credits to start; sign in when you want your boxes in the cloud.]

      Under it is FreshStack, the stack we build on: Cloudflare, GitHub, OpenRouter, Clerk, SpacetimeDB and LiveKit. Each piece is listed with its job and a documented way out, so nothing is forever.

      What isn't done yet: realtime multiplayer (that's next), spoken replies and payments. We'd rather tell you than have you find out.

      Two questions for you: what's the first thing you'd make it change about itself? And what broke?
